Chiefland Metal Roofing Questions

Who actually puts the roof on?

An independent, licensed roofing contractor who works in the Chiefland area. Mission Metals Manufacturing makes and supplies the metal roofing; the contractor installs it and contracts directly with you.

Who issues the roofing permit in Chiefland?

It depends on where the property sits. Addresses inside Chiefland city limits are permitted through the City of Chiefland, while homes in unincorporated Levy County go through the Levy County Building Department in Bronson. Your licensed installer confirms the right office and pulls the permit.

Can metal roofing go on a mobile or manufactured home?

Often, yes. Manufactured homes are common around Chiefland and rural Levy County, and metal roofing is a popular reroof choice for them. The installer checks the existing roof structure and recommends an approach that meets code for your home.

I'm on acreage with a barn and outbuildings. Can those be covered too?

Yes. Many Levy County properties include pole barns, equipment sheds and workshops. PBR or 5V crimp metal roofing is practical for outbuildings and can be color-matched to the house so the whole property looks consistent.

How does Nature Coast humidity affect a roof?

Chiefland sits inland from the Gulf on Florida's Nature Coast, where long, humid summers encourage algae and moisture problems on shingle roofs. Coated metal roofing sheds water quickly and doesn't hold moisture the way shingle granules do.

What about wind from Gulf storms?

Storms moving in off the Gulf can bring strong winds across Levy County. The Florida Building Code sets wind requirements by location and exposure, and your installer specifies the fastening pattern for your address.

How do I verify the installer's license?

Search the contractor's name or license number at myfloridalicense.com. We only refer contractors who hold an active Florida roofing license, and you can confirm it before you sign anything.

What areas around Chiefland do you cover?

We connect property owners in Chiefland and across Levy County, including Bronson, Fanning Springs, the Manatee Springs area and rural properties along the US-19 / US-98 corridor.
